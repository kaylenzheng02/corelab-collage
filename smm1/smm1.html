SMM 1 (FIRS1)

From Dionatos et al. 2014, A&A 563, A28

Atomic jet from SMM1 (FIRS1) in Serpens uncovers protobinary companion

O. Dionatos, J. K. Jørgensen, P. S. Teixeira, M. Güdel, E. Bergin

A&A 563, A28 (2014) · DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/201322799

Overview

SMM1 (also called FIRS1) is the most prominent protostellar source in Serpens. Spitzer observations reveal an atomic jet in [FeII] and [NeII], showing that SMM1 is a protobinary: a deeply embedded Class 0 source (SMM1-a) and a more evolved Class I/II companion (SMM1-b) about 1.5 to 1.8″ to the northwest.

Key findings

  • SMM1 is two young stars: SMM1-a and SMM1-b.
  • Each drives its own jet, and the two jets point in different directions.
  • The atomic jet likely comes from SMM1-b; the molecular gas is linked to SMM1-a.
  • Larger cloud outflows also split into two systems, matching the binary picture.

Methods

Surroundings of SMM1 were mapped with the Spitzer Infrared Spectrograph (IRS) in slit-scan mode (SH and LH modules, ~10 to 37 μm). Molecular (H2) and atomic ([FeII], [NeII], [SiII], [SI]) emission were compared with deconvolved Spitzer IRAC/MIPS images and high-velocity CO J = 3-2 outflow maps. Physical conditions were assessed with LTE, non-LTE, and shock models.

Conclusion

The Spitzer atomic jet disentangles the complex outflow morphology around SMM1 into two precessing outflows. Morphological and physical properties show SMM1 is a protobinary: the companion to the embedded Class 0 protostar SMM1-a is a more evolved Class I/II source (SMM1-b).
